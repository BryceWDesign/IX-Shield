# IX-Shield Cross Section Baseline v1

## Purpose
This document describes the baseline conceptual cross section used for the first IX-Shield proof-of-concept screening pass.

The purpose of this cross section is not to freeze a final hardware design.
The purpose is to define a stable, inspectable baseline for early analysis and discussion.

## Baseline Cross-Section Intent
Baseline v1 is designed to express the following ideas:

- the outer boundary is not treated as the sole shielding answer;
- the concept relies on layered, multifunctional protection logic;
- hydrogen-rich mass is deliberately centered in the shielding strategy;
- the inner occupied side remains part of the concept rather than an afterthought.

## Conceptual Layer Order
From exterior to interior, the baseline conceptual order is:

1. outer functional boundary;
2. stand-off or protective intermediate region;
3. hydrogen-rich primary shielding layer;
4. inner support / liner layer;
5. crew-facing interior boundary.

This order is conceptual and used to anchor analysis assumptions.
It does not imply that every real implementation must use identical fabrication methods.

## Layer Descriptions

### Layer 1 — Outer Functional Boundary
Role:
- first physical exterior boundary;
- service surface;
- foundation for environmental exposure logic.

Design intent:
- durable boundary role;
- not relied on as the dominant deep-space crew-radiation answer.

### Layer 2 — Stand-Off / Protective Intermediate Region
Role:
- spacing and multifunctional separation;
- future compatibility with protective and integration functions;
- support for non-monolithic wall logic.

Design intent:
- create geometric and functional separation;
- allow future variants to host stuffed-panel or MMOD-oriented logic.

### Layer 3 — Primary Hydrogen-Rich Shielding Layer
Role:
- main passive shielding contributor in the baseline concept;
- principal low-Z, hydrogen-rich mass region.

Design intent:
- provide concept-stage shielding mass in a form aligned with a physically serious low-Z strategy;
- avoid defaulting to dense blanket metal as the core answer.

### Layer 4 — Inner Support / Liner Layer
Role:
- stabilize the stack conceptually;
- separate main shielding mass from the immediate crew-facing boundary;
- provide a place for future tuned interlayer logic if justified.

Design intent:
- create a rational interior transition;
- support maintainability and bounded layer logic.

### Layer 5 — Crew-Facing Interior Boundary
Role:
- final inhabited-side surface;
- part of the occupied environmental boundary.

Design intent:
- represent the real crew-facing side of the architecture rather than ending the concept at a raw shielding block.

## What This Baseline Is Meant to Represent
This baseline is meant to represent:

- a transit-shell concept;
- a rational starting point for areal-density screening;
- a mass-first, low-Z-biased architecture;
- a platform for later storm-shelter and zone-mass variants.

## What This Baseline Is Not
This baseline is not:

- a manufacturing drawing;
- a qualification-ready section;
- a transport-validated final stack;
- a medical safety guarantee;
- a claim of full protection.

## Why The Baseline Is Deliberately Conservative
Baseline v1 is intentionally conservative in presentation.

It avoids:
- overcomplicated speculative features in the first section definition;
- dense-metal blanket assumptions;
- claims that geometry or layering alone solve all crew-radiation problems.

This helps preserve a clean line between:
- what is currently defined;
- what may be explored later;
- what must be proven before being claimed.

## Relationship To Shelter Variants
The baseline cross section is not the storm shelter.

Instead, it acts as the parent architecture that can be locally enhanced through:
- additional water-equivalent mass;
- denser local protection around a smaller refuge volume;
- reconfigurable mission-mass placement near crew.

## Relationship To Zone Mass Logic
The baseline also supports zone-level reasoning.

The same cross section may perform differently in practice depending on:
- where the crew volume sits;
- what logistics mass surrounds it;
- whether a given wall is adjacent to equipment or open space;
- whether the local geometry is central or exposed.

Because of this, IX-Shield treats baseline wall logic and zone geometry as linked, not independent.

## Review Standard
A reviewer should treat this baseline as acceptable for concept-stage work if it is:

- coherent;
- bounded;
- reviewable;
- easy to compare against future variants.

A reviewer should reject the baseline if it is used to imply validation or operational safety beyond what the repository actually proves.

## Summary
Cross Section Baseline v1 defines the starting physical logic of IX-Shield:
a layered, multifunctional, hydrogen-rich-biased crew-shell concept intended for early architecture screening and disciplined refinement.
